About Me

I am an Assistant Professor of Artificial Intelligence at the University of Nevada, Reno, where I lead MAGI Lab.

I develop reliable and interpretable AI methods for visual, multimodal, scientific, and socially meaningful data, with applications in medical AI, geometric intelligence, and AI for Good.

Research at MAGI

  • Trustworthy and Agentic AI: Evidence-grounded reasoning with language models and retrieval-augmented systems.
  • AI for Scientific Discovery: Connecting scientific data and domain knowledge to support research and decision-making.
  • Medical and Multimodal AI: Interpretable learning across medical images, clinical text, and time series.
  • Geometric Learning: Using geometry and topology to capture meaningful structure in data.

Research

At MAGI Lab, we develop AI methods that learn from structure, connect information across modalities, and support reasoning that can be examined and evaluated. Our work brings together geometric learning, trustworthy AI, and scientific applications, with a broader commitment to AI for Good.

Trustworthy and Agentic AI

How can AI systems reason over complex information while remaining grounded in evidence?

We study language models, retrieval-augmented generation, and agentic workflows that help people work with complex scientific and technical information. We are interested in connecting model outputs to supporting sources, evaluating reasoning, and making limitations visible to the experts who use these systems.

Related workHyperbolic embeddings for retrieval-augmented generation

AI for Scientific Discovery

How can AI connect scientific data and domain knowledge to support discovery and decision-making?

Scientific problems require more than predictions: they require reasoning across measurements, models, literature, and domain constraints. We develop methods to support these workflows in collaboration with domain experts. Our DOE Genesis Mission project, Data to Decision, explores evidence-traceable AI reasoning for nuclear waste repository safety assessments; related work investigates AI-piloted critical mineral recovery from spent batteries.

Current projectsData to DecisionCritical mineral recovery

Medical and Multimodal AI

How can complementary sources of information help AI better represent clinically meaningful patterns?

We develop learning methods for medical images, clinical text, and time series, with an emphasis on interpretable representations and clinically informed modeling. In computational pathology, our work explores how multimodal experts and concept-guided learning can support pediatric brain tumor classification and make model behavior easier to inspect.

Selected papersClinically informed modelingPathMoEConceptM3oE

Geometric and Topological Learning

How can the structure of data guide what a model learns?

Geometry and topology provide tools for representing shape, relationships, and variation in data. We use these ideas to develop structure-aware representations, implicit neural models, and knowledge-distillation methods for visual and time series data. Our goal is to build learning methods whose representations reflect meaningful properties of the underlying problem.

Selected papersShape-aware implicit neural modelsTopological persistence for sensor data

Principal Investigator

Ankita Shukla

Bio

I am an Assistant Professor in the Computer Science and Engineering Department at University of Nevada Reno, USA. Before joining UNR, I was a Postdoctoral researcher in the Geometric Media Lab, working with Prof. Pavan Turaga at Arizona State University.

Research Interests

My research interests lie in deep learning and machine learning approaches for visual and time series data, topological data analysis, differential geometry, and geometry driven approaches for learning. From the applications point of view, I focus on AI for Science and AI for Good, specifically towards wildlife conservation and human health applications.
